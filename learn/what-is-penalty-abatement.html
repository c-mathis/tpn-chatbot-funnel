What Is Penalty Abatement?

Penalty abatement is IRS relief that reduces or removes tax penalties from your account, potentially saving you thousands of dollars on your tax bill.

IRS penalties can add up quickly and significantly increase your tax debt. If you've been assessed penalties for filing late, paying late, or other compliance issues, you may be able to have those penalties reduced or removed entirely through penalty abatement. This legitimate form of tax relief is often overlooked but can provide substantial savings.

Understanding IRS Penalties

The IRS assesses penalties for various compliance failures. The most common penalties that can be abated include:

Failure-to-File Penalty

This penalty applies when you don't file your tax return by the due date (including extensions). The penalty is 5% of unpaid taxes for each month or part of a month the return is late, up to a maximum of 25% of your unpaid taxes. This is one of the most expensive penalties and adds up quickly.

Failure-to-Pay Penalty

This penalty applies when you don't pay the taxes reported on your return by the due date. The penalty is 0.5% of unpaid taxes for each month or part of a month the tax remains unpaid, up to a maximum of 25%. If both penalties apply, the failure-to-file penalty is reduced by the failure-to-pay amount.

Failure-to-Deposit Penalty

This penalty applies to businesses that don't deposit employment taxes on time. The penalty ranges from 2% to 15% depending on how late the deposit is made.

Types of Penalty Abatement

First-Time Penalty Abatement (FTA)

First-Time Penalty Abatement is an administrative waiver the IRS provides to taxpayers who have a clean compliance history. It's one of the easiest forms of penalty relief to obtain if you qualify.

To qualify for FTA, you must:

  • Have no penalties (except estimated tax penalties) for the prior 3 tax years
  • Have filed all required returns or filed an extension
  • Have paid, or arranged to pay, any tax due

FTA applies to failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, and failure-to-deposit penalties. You can request it by calling the IRS or submitting a written request.

Reasonable Cause Abatement

If you don't qualify for First-Time Abatement, you may still be able to have penalties removed by demonstrating reasonable cause. This means you had a legitimate reason for failing to comply with tax obligations and exercised ordinary business care and prudence.

Circumstances that may constitute reasonable cause include:

  • Death, serious illness, or unavoidable absence
  • Fire, casualty, natural disaster, or other disturbance
  • Inability to obtain records
  • Incorrect advice from a tax professional or the IRS
  • Ignorance of the law (in limited circumstances)
  • Reasonable reliance on incorrect written IRS guidance

Statutory Exceptions

In some cases, the law itself provides penalty relief. For example, if you're due a refund, the failure-to-file penalty doesn't apply. Similarly, penalties may be waived during federally declared disasters.

How to Request Penalty Abatement

There are several ways to request penalty relief:

  1. Phone request: For First-Time Abatement, you can call the IRS at the number on your notice. Have your tax information ready and be prepared to confirm your compliance history.
  2. Written request: Send a letter to the IRS explaining why you believe penalties should be abated. Include your name, address, Social Security number, tax year, and specific reasons for your request.
  3. Form 843: Use Form 843 (Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement) for a formal written request.
  4. Professional representation: A tax professional can request abatement on your behalf and may achieve better results, especially for reasonable cause claims.

Penalty Abatement vs. Penalty Relief Programs

It's important to understand that penalty abatement is different from other IRS relief programs. An Offer in Compromise can reduce your total tax debt including penalties, while penalty abatement specifically removes or reduces just the penalty portion. Sometimes combining strategies provides the best outcome.

Key Points to Remember

Significant Savings

Penalties can add up to 50% of your tax debt. Abating them can save thousands and make your balance much more manageable.

First-Time Relief

If you have a clean 3-year compliance history, First-Time Penalty Abatement is almost automatic and easy to request.

Document Everything

For reasonable cause claims, thorough documentation of your circumstances is essential for approval.

Interest Usually Stays

While penalties can be abated, interest generally cannot be waived except in cases of IRS error or delay.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is First-Time Penalty Abatement?

First-Time Penalty Abatement (FTA) is an IRS administrative waiver that removes failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, and failure-to-deposit penalties for taxpayers with a clean compliance history. To qualify, you must have filed all required returns, have no penalties in the prior three years, and be current on payments or have an approved payment plan.

What qualifies as reasonable cause for penalty abatement?

Reasonable cause includes circumstances beyond your control that prevented tax compliance, such as serious illness or death in the family, natural disasters, inability to obtain records, incorrect advice from the IRS, or unavoidable absence. You must document the circumstance and show you exercised ordinary business care and prudence.

How much can I save with penalty abatement?

Penalty savings can be substantial. The failure-to-file penalty is 5% per month (up to 25%), and the failure-to-pay penalty is 0.5% per month (up to 25%). For a $50,000 tax debt, penalties alone could add $25,000 or more. Successfully abating these penalties can save thousands of dollars and significantly reduce your total balance owed.

Can I request penalty abatement over the phone?

Yes, for First-Time Penalty Abatement, you can often request relief by calling the IRS and speaking with a representative. Have your tax information ready and be prepared to confirm your compliance history. For reasonable cause requests, a written request with supporting documentation is typically required for best results.

Will interest be abated along with penalties?

Generally, no. The IRS rarely abates interest charges because interest is required by law. However, when penalties are abated, any interest that accrued specifically on those penalties will also be reduced. The interest on the underlying tax debt will remain. In rare cases involving IRS errors or delays, some interest may be abated.
