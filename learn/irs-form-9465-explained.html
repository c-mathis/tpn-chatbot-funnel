IRS Form 9465 Explained

Your complete guide to requesting an installment agreement and setting up a payment plan with the IRS.

What Is IRS Form 9465?

IRS Form 9465, officially titled "Installment Agreement Request," is your formal application to set up a monthly payment plan with the IRS. If you cannot pay your full tax bill when it's due, this form allows you to propose a structured repayment schedule that works within your budget.

An installment agreement is one of the most common solutions for taxpayers with IRS debt. Rather than facing aggressive collection actions like wage garnishment or bank levies, you make agreed-upon monthly payments until your balance is paid in full, including interest and penalties that continue to accrue.

Types of IRS Payment Plans

Not all installment agreements are the same. The type you qualify for depends on how much you owe and your ability to pay:

Guaranteed Installment Agreement

If you owe $10,000 or less (excluding penalties and interest), haven't had an installment agreement in the past 5 years, and can pay the balance within 36 months, the IRS must approve your request. You must also be current on all filing requirements and agree to file and pay on time going forward.

Streamlined Installment Agreement

For balances up to $50,000, you can request a streamlined agreement without providing detailed financial information. The IRS accepts these applications readily if you can pay within 72 months (6 years) or before the collection statute expires, whichever is shorter. Direct debit is required for balances between $25,000 and $50,000.

Non-Streamlined Installment Agreement

Owing more than $50,000 requires submitting Form 433-A (Collection Information Statement) to prove you cannot pay more than you're proposing. The IRS will evaluate your finances and determine the minimum acceptable payment amount.

Partial Payment Installment Agreement (PPIA)

If your monthly disposable income is too low to pay the full balance before the 10-year collection statute expires, you may qualify for a PPIA. You make payments based on what you can afford, and the remaining balance is forgiven when the statute expires. This requires detailed financial disclosure.

How to Complete Form 9465

Taxpayer Information (Lines 1-4)

Enter your name, Social Security Number, and current address exactly as they appear on your tax return. If filing jointly, both spouses' information is required. Include a daytime phone number where the IRS can reach you.

Tax Liability Information (Lines 5-8)

Specify the tax year and form for which you're requesting the agreement (e.g., "2023 Form 1040"). Enter the total amount you owe for that tax period. If you owe for multiple years, you can include them all on one Form 9465, listing each year and amount.

Payment Terms (Lines 9-11)

Propose your monthly payment amount and preferred payment date (any day from the 1st to the 28th of each month). Your payment must be realistic - for streamlined agreements, calculate by dividing your total balance by 72 months at minimum.

Bank Information for Direct Debit (Lines 12-14)

If you're setting up automatic payments (required for some agreements and recommended for lower fees), provide your bank routing number and account number. Direct debit reduces the risk of default and shows the IRS you're committed to the agreement.

Employment Information (Line 15)

Provide your employer's name, address, and phone number. This helps the IRS verify your income and contact you through your employer if necessary.

Setup Fees and Costs

The IRS charges a one-time setup fee for installment agreements:

  • $225: Standard setup fee when applying by mail or phone
  • $130: Direct debit agreements (automatic bank withdrawals)
  • $107: Online Payment Agreement applications
  • $43: Online applications with direct debit for low-income taxpayers
  • $31: Low-income taxpayers using direct debit (reimbursable under certain conditions)

To qualify for low-income rates, your income must be at or below 250% of the federal poverty level. The fee can be added to your balance or paid separately.

Online vs. Paper Application

For most taxpayers, applying online through IRS.gov is faster and cheaper:

Online Payment Agreement advantages:

  • Lower setup fees
  • Instant approval for qualifying applications
  • No need to mail forms or wait for processing
  • Easy to modify payment dates or amounts later
  • Available 24/7

When to use paper Form 9465:

  • You owe more than $50,000 (requires Form 433-A)
  • You're requesting a payroll deduction agreement
  • You prefer not to provide bank information online
  • You're filing jointly with Form 1040 and attaching the request

What Happens After You Apply

After submitting Form 9465, the IRS will review your request:

  1. Acknowledgment: You'll receive a notice confirming receipt, usually within 30 days.
  2. Review: The IRS verifies your information and determines if your proposal is acceptable.
  3. Approval or counter-offer: You'll receive notice of approval, rejection, or a modified payment amount.
  4. Begin payments: Once approved, start making payments by your agreed-upon date.

While your request is pending, the IRS generally won't take collection action. However, penalties and interest continue accruing on your balance.

Maintaining Your Agreement

Once approved, you must follow these rules to keep your agreement in good standing:

  • Make all payments on time: Late or missed payments can result in default.
  • File all tax returns: You must file your returns by the due date each year.
  • Pay current taxes: New tax liabilities must be paid in full when due.
  • Update contact information: Notify the IRS if you move or change phone numbers.
  • Avoid new tax debt: Incurring additional tax debt can void your agreement.

What If Your Circumstances Change?

Life happens. If you can no longer afford your agreed payments:

  • Request a modification: Contact the IRS to lower your payment amount (may require updated financial information).
  • Request a skip: You may be able to skip one or two payments in cases of temporary hardship.
  • Consider other options: If your financial situation has significantly worsened, an Offer in Compromise or Currently Not Collectible status might be more appropriate.

Key Points to Remember

Apply Online to Save

Online applications have lower setup fees ($107 vs. $225) and often receive faster approval than mailed forms.

Direct Debit Recommended

Automatic payments reduce your setup fee and virtually eliminate the risk of missed payments that could default your agreement.

72-Month Maximum

Streamlined agreements require paying your balance within 72 months. Longer terms require detailed financial disclosure.

Stay Compliant

You must file all returns on time and pay current taxes to keep your installment agreement active.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is IRS Form 9465?

IRS Form 9465 is the Installment Agreement Request form used to apply for a monthly payment plan to pay off your tax debt over time. It allows taxpayers who cannot pay their full tax liability at once to make manageable monthly payments instead of facing collection actions like wage garnishment or bank levies.

How much does it cost to set up an installment agreement?

Setup fees vary based on how you apply and your payment method. Standard mail/phone applications cost $225, online applications cost $107, and direct debit agreements are $130 (or $43 online). Low-income taxpayers may qualify for reduced fees of $43 or $31. These fees can be added to your balance.

What is the minimum payment for an IRS installment agreement?

For guaranteed installment agreements (debts under $10,000), your monthly payment must pay off the balance within 36 months. For streamlined agreements (debts under $50,000), payments must clear the balance within 72 months. Larger debts require financial analysis using Form 433-A to determine the minimum acceptable payment.

Can I set up an installment agreement online?

Yes, if you owe $50,000 or less in combined tax, penalties, and interest, you can apply online through the IRS Online Payment Agreement tool at IRS.gov. Online applications are typically processed faster and have lower setup fees than mailing Form 9465.

What happens if I miss a payment on my installment agreement?

Missing a payment puts your agreement at risk of default. The IRS may terminate the agreement and resume collection actions including levies and liens. If you anticipate trouble making a payment, contact the IRS immediately to request a modification, payment skip, or temporary hardship suspension before you miss the payment.
