What Is Tax Compliance?

Tax compliance is the foundation for any tax relief program. Understanding what compliance means, why it matters, and how to achieve it is essential for resolving your tax issues with the IRS.

Tax compliance refers to fulfilling all your legal obligations to file tax returns and pay taxes on time. For the IRS, compliance is not just about paying what you owe; it is about following the rules of the tax system. Being compliant is a prerequisite for virtually every tax relief program, making it the essential first step in resolving any tax debt.

What Does Tax Compliance Include?

To be considered tax compliant, you must meet several requirements:

1. All Required Returns Filed

You must file all tax returns that you were legally required to file. For most individuals, this means:

  • Personal income tax returns (Form 1040) for all years where your income exceeded the filing threshold
  • Self-employment tax returns if you had net self-employment income of $400 or more
  • Business returns (corporate, partnership, or sole proprietorship schedules) if you operated a business
  • Employment tax returns (Form 941, 940) if you had employees
  • Information returns (1099s, W-2s) if required

2. Current on Estimated Tax Payments

If you receive income that is not subject to withholding (self-employment, investments, rental income), you may be required to make quarterly estimated tax payments. These are due:

  • Q1: April 15
  • Q2: June 15
  • Q3: September 15
  • Q4: January 15 of the following year

Being current means you have paid all required estimated tax installments for the current year.

3. Current on Payroll Tax Deposits

If you have employees, you must be current on all federal payroll tax deposits. This includes making timely deposits of withheld income tax and FICA taxes according to your deposit schedule (monthly or semi-weekly based on your total liability).

Why Compliance Matters for Tax Relief

The IRS will not approve most tax relief programs until you are compliant. Here is why:

Offer in Compromise

You cannot submit an Offer in Compromise until all required returns are filed. Even if your offer is accepted, you must remain compliant for 5 years or the offer will default and the original debt will be reinstated.

Installment Agreement

The IRS will not set up an installment agreement until you have filed all required returns. If you default on filing requirements during the agreement, it can be terminated.

Currently Not Collectible Status

Before placing your account in CNC status, the IRS requires all returns to be filed so they can assess the full extent of your liability.

Penalty Abatement

While penalty abatement may be possible for late filing penalties, you must still file the returns before requesting abatement.

The 6-Year Filing Requirement

A common question is how far back you need to go when catching up on unfiled returns. The IRS has a general policy requiring at least 6 years of compliance, meaning:

  • The most recent 6 years of returns should be filed
  • Additional years may be required in some circumstances
  • Returns more than 6 years old are sometimes not required, but this is case-by-case

Getting back into compliance often involves reconstructing records, obtaining wage and income transcripts, and preparing returns for multiple years. This is where professional assistance can be invaluable.

Maintaining Compliance

Once you achieve compliance and receive tax relief, you must maintain it. Here are key practices:

For Individuals

  • File your return by April 15 each year (or request an extension)
  • Make estimated tax payments if you have non-wage income
  • Adjust your W-4 withholding if you consistently owe at year-end
  • Keep organized records throughout the year
  • Set aside money for taxes as you earn income

For Business Owners

  • Make all payroll tax deposits on time
  • File quarterly employment tax returns (Form 941)
  • Make estimated tax payments for your business income
  • Keep business and personal finances separate
  • Consider working with a bookkeeper or accountant

Getting Back Into Compliance

If you have unfiled returns or are behind on estimated payments, here are the steps to get back on track:

  1. Gather your records: Collect W-2s, 1099s, bank statements, receipts, and other documentation
  2. Request transcripts: Get wage and income transcripts from the IRS to identify unreported income
  3. Prepare returns: Complete all unfiled returns, even if you cannot pay the balance
  4. File the returns: Submit all returns to the IRS (electronically or by mail)
  5. Get current on estimated taxes: Begin making required estimated payments
  6. Address balances owed: Once compliant, explore tax relief options

The Fresh Start

Becoming compliant is not about punishment; it is about starting fresh. Once you are compliant, you qualify for programs designed to help you resolve past debt and move forward. The IRS recognizes that circumstances change and people fall behind. The compliance requirement ensures that as you resolve old debt, you are not creating new problems.

Key Points to Remember

All Returns Filed

You must file all required returns for at least the past 6 years to qualify for tax relief programs.

Current on Payments

Estimated tax payments and payroll deposits must be current for the current tax year.

Prerequisite for Relief

OICs, installment agreements, and CNC status all require compliance before approval.

5-Year Requirement

After an OIC is accepted, you must stay compliant for 5 years or risk defaulting the agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean to be tax compliant?

Being tax compliant means you have filed all required tax returns and are current with any required estimated tax payments or payroll tax deposits. For individuals, this typically means having filed all personal income tax returns for at least the past 6 years and being current on the current year's estimated taxes if applicable.

Why is tax compliance required for tax relief?

The IRS requires compliance as a condition of most tax relief programs. They will not grant an Offer in Compromise, installment agreement, or currently not collectible status if you have unfiled returns. This ensures taxpayers are not accumulating new debt while trying to resolve old debt, and allows the IRS to accurately assess your total tax liability.

How many years of tax returns do I need to file?

The IRS generally requires the last 6 years of unfiled returns to be filed before granting tax relief. However, depending on your situation, a Revenue Officer may require more or accept fewer years. The IRS policy is to ensure all returns within the 6-year enforcement period are filed.

What are estimated tax payments?

Estimated tax payments are quarterly payments made by individuals who receive income not subject to withholding, such as self-employment income, investment income, or rental income. These payments are due April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15. If you are required to make estimated payments, you must be current on them to be considered compliant.

What happens if I fall out of compliance after getting tax relief?

Most tax relief agreements require you to remain compliant for a period after approval, typically 5 years for an Offer in Compromise. If you fail to file returns or pay taxes during this period, your agreement may be defaulted, and the original debt (minus any payments made) could be reinstated with additional penalties and interest.
