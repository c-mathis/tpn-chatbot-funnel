What Is an Offer in Compromise? A Complete Guide

Learn how an Offer in Compromise works, who qualifies, and how it could help you settle your tax debt for less than you owe.

February 24, 2026 8 min read

If you owe more to the IRS than you can realistically pay, an Offer in Compromise (OIC) might be your path to a fresh start. This program allows qualifying taxpayers to settle their tax debt for less than the full amount owed.

What Is an Offer in Compromise?

An Offer in Compromise is an agreement between a taxpayer and the Internal Revenue Service that settles a tax liability for less than the full amount owed. It's designed to be a legitimate option for taxpayers who genuinely cannot pay their full tax debt.

The IRS will generally accept an OIC when the amount offered represents the most they can expect to collect within a reasonable period of time. This is determined by evaluating your:

  • Ability to pay
  • Income and expenses
  • Asset equity
  • Future earning potential

Who Qualifies for an Offer in Compromise?

Not everyone qualifies for an OIC. The IRS considers several factors when evaluating your application:

Basic Eligibility Requirements

Before the IRS will consider your offer, you must:

  • Have filed all required tax returns
  • Have made all required estimated tax payments for the current year
  • Not be in an open bankruptcy proceeding
  • Have a valid extension for the current year's return (if applying during tax season)

How the OIC Process Works

The Offer in Compromise process involves several steps:

1. Determine Your Offer Amount

The IRS uses a specific formula to calculate the minimum offer they'll accept. This is based on your "reasonable collection potential" (RCP), which includes:

  • Asset equity: The value of your assets minus any loans
  • Future income: Your expected disposable income over a set period

2. Submit Your Application

You'll need to complete Form 656 (Offer in Compromise) and Form 433-A (OIC) or 433-B (OIC) for business taxes. There's also a $205 application fee and an initial payment (which varies based on your payment option).

3. Wait for IRS Review

The IRS typically takes 6-12 months to review an OIC application. During this time, they may request additional documentation or clarification.

4. Receive a Decision

The IRS will either accept, reject, or return your offer. If rejected, you have 30 days to appeal the decision.

Payment Options

The IRS offers two payment options for accepted offers:

Lump Sum Cash Offer

Pay 20% of the total offer amount with your application, then pay the remaining balance in five or fewer payments within five months of acceptance.

Periodic Payment Offer

Make monthly payments while the IRS considers your offer, then continue monthly payments for up to 24 months after acceptance.

Pros and Cons of an Offer in Compromise

Advantages

  • Settle your debt for less than you owe
  • Stop collection actions while your offer is being considered
  • Get a fresh start with the IRS
  • End the stress of unmanageable tax debt

Disadvantages

  • Lengthy application process (6-12 months)
  • Non-refundable application fee
  • Must stay compliant with tax obligations for 5 years after acceptance
  • Tax refunds may be applied to your debt during the process

Tips for a Successful OIC Application

  1. Be completely honest: The IRS will verify all information you provide
  2. Document everything: Provide thorough documentation of your financial situation
  3. Consider professional help: A tax professional can help navigate the complex process
  4. Stay current: Continue filing returns and paying current taxes during the process

Is an Offer in Compromise Right for You?

An OIC can be a powerful tool for resolving tax debt, but it's not the right solution for everyone. If you can pay your full tax liability through an installment agreement or other means, the IRS will likely reject your offer.

The best way to determine if you qualify is to evaluate your complete financial picture and understand all available options.
