What Is IRS Form 8821?

IRS Form 8821, officially titled "Tax Information Authorization," allows you to designate individuals or organizations to receive and inspect your confidential tax information from the IRS. This form provides a way to share your tax data with third parties without giving them the authority to represent you or take actions on your behalf.

Think of Form 8821 as granting "view-only" access to your tax records. The person or entity you designate can request transcripts, review account information, and receive copies of notices sent to you - but they cannot speak on your behalf, negotiate with the IRS, or sign any documents for you.

When Would You Use Form 8821?

Form 8821 is useful in many situations where someone needs access to your tax information but doesn't need to represent you:

Mortgage and Loan Applications

Lenders often require verification of income and tax filing status. Form 8821 allows your mortgage company or bank to directly obtain tax transcripts from the IRS to verify the information on your application.

Financial Planning

Financial advisors, wealth managers, or estate planners may need to review your tax returns and account status to provide comprehensive financial advice. Form 8821 lets them access this information without you having to gather and share documents manually.

Family Monitoring

Adult children may want to help elderly parents manage their finances. Form 8821 allows family members to receive tax notices and information to ensure nothing falls through the cracks.

Business Purposes

Partners, investors, or potential buyers may need to verify a business's tax compliance. Form 8821 provides controlled access to specific tax information.

Initial Tax Professional Review

Before fully engaging a tax professional with Power of Attorney, you might use Form 8821 to let them review your tax situation and provide a preliminary assessment.

Form 8821 vs. Form 2848: Key Differences

Understanding the difference between these two forms is crucial for choosing the right authorization:

Capability Form 8821 Form 2848
Receive tax information Yes Yes
Request transcripts Yes Yes
Speak with IRS on your behalf No Yes
Sign documents for you No Yes
Negotiate with IRS No Yes
Who can be authorized Anyone Qualified practitioners only

Key insight: Form 8821 is for information sharing; Form 2848 is for representation. If you need someone to handle your tax matters, not just view them, you need Form 2848.

How to Complete Form 8821

Line 1: Taxpayer Information

Enter your name, current address, and taxpayer identification number (Social Security Number for individuals, EIN for businesses). Include your telephone and fax numbers. If married and filing jointly, you can include your spouse's information to authorize access to joint information.

Line 2: Appointee Information

Provide detailed information about each person or entity you're authorizing:

  • Full name
  • Mailing address
  • CAF number (if the appointee has one from previous IRS dealings)
  • Telephone number
  • Fax number

You can list multiple appointees on the same form.

Line 3: Tax Information

Specify exactly what tax information the appointee can access:

  • Type of Tax: Income, employment, estate, gift, excise, etc.
  • Tax Form Number: 1040, 941, 1120, etc.
  • Year(s) or Period(s): Specific years or "all years"
  • Specific Tax Matters: If you want to limit access further (optional)

Be precise - the appointee can only access the exact information you specify.

Line 4: Specific Use

Check this box if the authorization is for a specific, one-time use and shouldn't be recorded on the IRS's Centralized Authorization File (CAF). This is rare - most people leave this unchecked.

Line 5: Retention/Revocation

Check this box if you want to keep any prior Form 8821 authorizations in effect. Otherwise, filing a new Form 8821 automatically revokes prior ones for the same tax matters.

Line 6: Taxpayer Signature

Sign and date the form. If filing for a business, an authorized officer must sign. For joint returns, only one spouse needs to sign unless both are granting separate authorizations.

How to Submit Form 8821

You have several submission options:

  • Fax: Fax to the IRS CAF unit for your state. This is usually the fastest method.
  • Mail: Send to the IRS address for your state. Allow several weeks for processing.
  • Online: Tax professionals can submit through their Tax Pro Account.

What Information Can Appointees Access?

Depending on what you authorize, your appointee can access:

  • Tax return transcripts
  • Account transcripts showing balances and payments
  • Wage and income transcripts
  • Record of account (detailed transaction history)
  • Copies of notices and correspondence
  • Verification of non-filing letters

Revoking or Modifying Form 8821

You maintain full control over your authorizations:

To Revoke

Send a written statement to the IRS CAF unit that includes:

  • Your name and taxpayer identification number
  • The appointee's name
  • The tax matters being revoked
  • A clear statement that you're revoking the authorization
  • Your signature and date

To Modify

Submit a new Form 8821 with the updated authorization. Unless you check the retention box, this automatically revokes the prior authorization for the same matters.

Important Considerations

  • Not for representation: Form 8821 appointees cannot advocate for you, negotiate, or take any actions on your behalf.
  • Confidentiality: Consider carefully who you authorize. They'll have access to sensitive financial information.
  • Specific authorization: Only authorize access to information the appointee actually needs.
  • Duration: The authorization doesn't expire unless you specify an end date or revoke it.
  • Joint filers: Authorization for a joint return requires explicit specification if you want both spouses' information shared.

Key Points to Remember

View-Only Access

Form 8821 allows others to see your tax information but not act on it. They cannot represent you or make decisions.

Authorize Anyone

Unlike Form 2848, you can authorize any individual or organization - family members, lenders, advisors, or anyone else.

You Stay in Control

Revoke or modify your authorization at any time. The IRS will stop sharing information as soon as revocation is processed.

Specific Information Only

You control exactly what tax years and types of information the appointee can access. Be specific in Line 3.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is IRS Form 8821?

IRS Form 8821 is the Tax Information Authorization form that allows you to designate individuals or organizations to receive and inspect your confidential tax information from the IRS. Unlike Form 2848 (Power of Attorney), Form 8821 does not authorize anyone to represent you or take actions on your behalf - it only grants access to view your tax records.

Who can I authorize with Form 8821?

Unlike Form 2848 which requires qualified practitioners, Form 8821 can authorize anyone you trust - including family members, friends, financial advisors, mortgage lenders, accountants, or any individual or organization that needs access to your tax information for legitimate purposes. There are no professional licensing requirements.

What is the difference between Form 8821 and Form 2848?

Form 8821 only allows access to tax information - the designee cannot represent you, make decisions, sign documents, or take any actions on your behalf. Form 2848 (Power of Attorney) grants full representation authority, allowing your representative to speak with the IRS, negotiate, sign documents, and handle matters for you. Use 8821 for information sharing, 2848 for representation.

When would I use Form 8821 instead of Form 2848?

Use Form 8821 when you need someone to access your tax records but not represent you - such as when applying for a mortgage, sharing information with a financial planner, allowing a family member to monitor your account, or letting a potential business partner verify tax compliance. Use Form 2848 when you need someone to actually handle tax matters on your behalf.

How long does Form 8821 remain valid?

Form 8821 remains in effect until you revoke it, submit a new Form 8821 for the same matters (which automatically revokes the prior one), or the authorization expires on a date you specified when completing the form. You can revoke it at any time by sending written notice to the IRS CAF unit.
