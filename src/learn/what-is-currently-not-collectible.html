When you owe taxes but truly cannot afford to pay anything without sacrificing basic necessities like food, housing, or utilities, the IRS may place your account in Currently Not Collectible status. This designation acknowledges that collection would be futile and provides temporary relief from IRS enforcement actions.

How Does Currently Not Collectible Status Work?

Currently Not Collectible status is not a program you apply for in the traditional sense. Instead, it's a determination the IRS makes after reviewing your financial situation and concluding that you have no ability to pay your tax debt without experiencing economic hardship.

When your account is placed in CNC status:

  • The IRS stops sending collection notices and making collection calls
  • Levies on your wages, bank accounts, and other assets are paused
  • Your case is temporarily closed from active collection
  • The 10-year collection statute continues to run
  • Interest and penalties continue to accrue on your balance

Who Qualifies for CNC Status?

The IRS grants Currently Not Collectible status to taxpayers who demonstrate genuine financial hardship. You may qualify if:

Your Income Doesn't Cover Basic Expenses

The IRS uses national and local standards to determine allowable living expenses. If your income minus these allowable expenses leaves nothing for tax payments, you may qualify. Basic expenses include housing, utilities, food, transportation, healthcare, and other necessities.

You're Experiencing Hardship Circumstances

Situations that commonly lead to CNC status include:

  • Unemployment or significant income reduction
  • Serious illness or disability
  • Living on Social Security or disability benefits alone
  • Fixed income that doesn't cover basic needs
  • Natural disaster or emergency situations

How to Request CNC Status

To request Currently Not Collectible status, you'll need to prove your financial hardship to the IRS:

  1. Gather financial documentation: Collect recent pay stubs, bank statements, proof of expenses, and any documentation of hardship circumstances
  2. Complete Form 433-F: This Collection Information Statement details your income, expenses, and assets
  3. Contact the IRS: Call the number on your most recent IRS notice or have a tax professional contact them on your behalf
  4. Provide supporting documents: Be prepared to fax or mail documentation supporting your hardship claim
  5. Wait for determination: The IRS will review your information and make a decision

What Happens While You're in CNC Status?

Understanding what CNC status does and doesn't do is important:

Your Debt Doesn't Go Away

CNC status pauses collection, but your tax debt still exists. Interest continues to accrue at the current rate, and the failure-to-pay penalty continues to accumulate (up to the 25% maximum). Your total balance will continue to grow while in CNC status.

Tax Liens May Still Apply

The IRS may file a Notice of Federal Tax Lien even if you're in CNC status. This protects the government's interest in your assets and creates a public record of your debt. The lien can affect your credit score and ability to sell property or obtain financing.

Refunds Will Be Applied to Your Balance

If you're owed a tax refund while in CNC status, the IRS will apply it to your outstanding balance. This is called an offset. To avoid losing refunds, adjust your withholding so you don't overpay during the year.

The Collection Statute Keeps Running

The IRS generally has 10 years from the date of assessment to collect a tax debt. This clock continues running while you're in CNC status. If your debt remains uncollected when the statute expires, it may be written off.

Periodic Reviews

CNC status isn't necessarily permanent. The IRS periodically reviews accounts in CNC status to determine if the taxpayer's financial situation has improved. If your income increases significantly, the IRS may remove you from CNC status and resume collection efforts or require you to enter into a payment plan.

Key Points to Remember

Collection Pause

CNC status stops IRS levies, garnishments, and collection calls, giving you breathing room during financial hardship.

Debt Remains

Your tax debt isn't forgiven in CNC status. Interest and penalties continue to accrue on your balance.

Statute Runs

The 10-year collection period continues during CNC status. If it expires, remaining debt may be forgiven.

Periodic Review

The IRS reviews CNC accounts periodically. If your finances improve, they may resume collection efforts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Currently Not Collectible status forgive my tax debt?

No, CNC status does not forgive your tax debt. It only pauses IRS collection efforts. Your debt continues to exist, and interest and penalties keep accruing. However, if your debt remains in CNC status until the 10-year collection statute expires, the remaining balance may be written off and you would no longer owe it.

How do I prove financial hardship to the IRS for CNC status?

You must complete Form 433-F (Collection Information Statement) documenting your income, expenses, and assets. The IRS compares your income against allowable living expenses. If your expenses equal or exceed your income, leaving nothing for tax payments, you may qualify for CNC status. You'll need to provide supporting documents like pay stubs, bank statements, and bills.

Will the IRS still file a tax lien if I'm in Currently Not Collectible status?

Yes, the IRS may still file a federal tax lien against your property even if you're granted CNC status. The lien protects the government's interest in your assets. While the IRS won't actively pursue levies or seizures during CNC status, the lien remains and can affect your credit and ability to sell property.

How long does Currently Not Collectible status last?

CNC status can last indefinitely as long as your financial hardship continues. However, the IRS periodically reviews CNC accounts (typically every 1-2 years) to see if your situation has improved. If your income increases significantly, they may remove you from CNC status and resume collection efforts or set up a payment plan.

Can I apply for an Offer in Compromise while in CNC status?

Yes, you can apply for an Offer in Compromise while in CNC status. In fact, being in CNC status may help your OIC case by demonstrating your inability to pay. Some taxpayers use CNC as a temporary solution while preparing an OIC application. A tax professional can help determine if transitioning from CNC to an OIC makes sense for your situation.
