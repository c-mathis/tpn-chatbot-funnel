Tax hardship occurs when paying your tax debt would prevent you from meeting basic living expenses. The IRS recognizes that some taxpayers genuinely cannot pay without causing serious financial harm to themselves and their families. For these taxpayers, the IRS offers several relief options designed to prevent undue hardship while still working toward resolution.

What Qualifies as Financial Hardship?

The IRS has specific criteria for determining financial hardship. Generally, you're considered in hardship when your monthly income, minus necessary living expenses, leaves nothing (or very little) available to pay toward your tax debt.

The IRS evaluates hardship based on:

  • Income: All sources including wages, self-employment, Social Security, disability, rental income, and investments
  • Necessary expenses: Housing, utilities, food, transportation, health care, child care, and other essential costs
  • Assets: Equity in your home, vehicles, retirement accounts, and other property
  • Future earning potential: Your ability to increase income or reduce expenses

The IRS uses Collection Financial Standards, which set allowable amounts for different expense categories based on your location and family size. These standards help ensure consistent treatment across taxpayers.

Currently Not Collectible (CNC) Status

The primary hardship relief is Currently Not Collectible status. When the IRS places your account in CNC, they temporarily halt collection activities because collecting would create financial hardship.

While in CNC status:

  • The IRS will not levy your wages or bank accounts
  • You won't receive collection calls or threatening letters
  • The 10-year collection statute continues to run
  • Interest and penalties continue to accrue (the debt still grows)
  • Federal tax liens may remain on your property
  • Tax refunds will be applied to your debt

The IRS periodically reviews CNC accounts (usually every 1-2 years) to determine if your financial situation has improved. If it has, they may resume collection or offer other resolution options.

How Hardship Affects Other Relief Programs

Financial hardship is a key factor in qualifying for several IRS relief programs:

Offer in Compromise: If hardship means you can't pay your full tax debt within the collection period, the IRS may accept an offer for less. Your hardship situation directly impacts the amount they'll accept.

Partial Pay Installment Agreement: If you can afford some payment but not enough to pay the full balance before the statute expires, you may qualify for a partial pay agreement. Hardship documentation supports reduced payment amounts.

Reasonable Cause Penalty Relief: If circumstances causing hardship also prevented you from paying on time, you may qualify for penalty abatement based on reasonable cause.

Levy Release: If you're currently under levy and it's causing hardship, the IRS may release the levy while you work out a resolution.

Documenting Your Hardship

The IRS requires documentation to verify your financial situation. You'll typically need:

Income documentation:

  • Recent pay stubs (usually last 2-3 months)
  • Social Security or disability benefit statements
  • Self-employment income records
  • Unemployment compensation statements
  • Any other income sources

Asset documentation:

  • Bank statements for all accounts (last 3 months)
  • Investment and retirement account statements
  • Property deeds and mortgage statements
  • Vehicle titles and loan documents

Expense documentation:

  • Rent or mortgage payment records
  • Utility bills
  • Medical bills and insurance premiums
  • Child care costs
  • Other regular monthly obligations

The IRS collects this information using Form 433-A (Collection Information Statement for Wage Earners and Self-Employed Individuals) or Form 433-F (a simplified version for some situations).

IRS Collection Financial Standards

The IRS doesn't accept all claimed expenses at face value. They use Collection Financial Standards to set maximum allowable amounts:

National Standards: Set amounts for food, clothing, housekeeping supplies, personal care, and miscellaneous expenses. These vary by income level and family size.

Local Standards: Housing and utility allowances that vary by county. Transportation costs that vary by region.

Conditional Expenses: Some expenses may be allowed beyond standard amounts if you can demonstrate necessity. Common examples include:

  • Medical expenses exceeding the standard
  • Child support or alimony payments
  • Payments on secured debts (to avoid losing essential assets)
  • Education expenses necessary for employment

Common Hardship Situations

The IRS regularly works with taxpayers facing various hardship circumstances:

  • Job loss or reduced income: Unemployment, reduced hours, or forced retirement
  • Medical issues: Serious illness, disability, or high medical expenses
  • Family circumstances: Divorce, death of spouse, or caring for ill family members
  • Natural disasters: Loss of property or income due to disasters
  • Fixed income: Retirees on Social Security with limited ability to increase income
  • Business failure: Self-employed individuals whose businesses have closed

Temporary vs. Permanent Hardship

The IRS distinguishes between temporary and permanent hardship:

Temporary hardship: Your situation is expected to improve (recovering from illness, finding new employment, business recovery). The IRS may place you in CNC temporarily and revisit later.

Permanent hardship: Your situation is unlikely to improve (permanent disability, advanced age, chronic health conditions). These cases may remain in CNC indefinitely, potentially until the collection statute expires.

Understanding which category you fall into helps determine the best resolution strategy. Temporary hardship might lead to CNC now with a payment plan later. Permanent hardship might make you an excellent candidate for an Offer in Compromise.

Key Points to Remember

Basic Expenses Protected

The IRS won't force you to choose between paying taxes and meeting basic needs like food, housing, and medical care.

Documentation Required

You'll need to prove hardship with income, expense, and asset documentation using IRS Form 433-A or 433-F.

CNC Stops Collection

Currently Not Collectible status pauses levies and garnishments while you're experiencing hardship.

Improves OIC Eligibility

Financial hardship is a key factor in qualifying for Offer in Compromise and settling for less than owed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What qualifies as financial hardship for the IRS?

The IRS considers you in financial hardship when paying your tax debt would prevent you from meeting basic living expenses like food, housing, utilities, transportation, and medical care. They use Collection Financial Standards to determine allowable expenses based on your location and family size. If your income minus allowable expenses leaves nothing to pay toward taxes, you may qualify for hardship status.

What is Currently Not Collectible status?

Currently Not Collectible (CNC) status is when the IRS temporarily stops collection efforts because paying would cause financial hardship. While in CNC, the IRS won't levy your wages or bank accounts, garnish your wages, or send collection notices. However, interest and penalties continue to accrue, tax liens may remain in place, and the IRS will intercept any tax refunds. The IRS reviews CNC accounts periodically to see if your financial situation has improved.

What documentation do I need to prove hardship?

To prove financial hardship, you'll typically need: recent pay stubs or proof of income, bank statements for all accounts (usually last 3 months), monthly bills and expense documentation, mortgage or rent statements, vehicle loan documentation, medical expense records, and any documentation of special circumstances (disability, medical conditions, job loss). The IRS uses Form 433-A (individuals) or 433-B (businesses) to collect this information.

Can hardship help me qualify for an Offer in Compromise?

Yes, financial hardship is one of the primary reasons people qualify for an Offer in Compromise. If your income and assets show you cannot reasonably pay your full tax debt within the collection period, the IRS may accept an offer for less. Your "Reasonable Collection Potential" calculation considers your hardship circumstances. Documented hardship can significantly reduce the amount the IRS will accept to settle your debt.

Does the IRS have different hardship standards?

Yes, the IRS uses Collection Financial Standards that vary by location and family size. These standards include national limits for food, clothing, and miscellaneous expenses; regional housing and utility allowances based on county; and local transportation costs. For expenses exceeding these standards, you must demonstrate necessity. Medical expenses and certain other costs may be allowed beyond standard amounts with documentation.
