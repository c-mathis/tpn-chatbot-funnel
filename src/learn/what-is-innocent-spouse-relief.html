When you file a joint tax return, both spouses become jointly and severally liable for the entire tax debt. This means the IRS can collect the full amount from either spouse, regardless of who earned the income or made the error. Innocent Spouse Relief provides protection when it would be unfair to hold you responsible for tax problems caused by your spouse.

Understanding Joint and Several Liability

Joint and several liability is a legal concept that makes both spouses equally responsible for the entire tax liability on a joint return. Even if:

  • Your spouse earned all the income
  • Your spouse prepared the return without your input
  • You didn't know about unreported income or false deductions
  • You're now divorced and the decree says your ex is responsible

The IRS can still pursue you for the full amount. A divorce decree assigning tax debt to your ex-spouse does not bind the IRS - they can collect from either party.

Three Types of Innocent Spouse Relief

1. Traditional Innocent Spouse Relief (IRC Section 6015(b))

This relief applies when there's an understatement of tax on your joint return due to erroneous items from your spouse. To qualify, you must show:

  • You filed a joint return with an understatement of tax
  • The understatement is due to erroneous items of your spouse
  • When you signed the return, you didn't know and had no reason to know there was an understatement
  • Considering all facts and circumstances, it would be unfair to hold you liable

2. Separation of Liability Relief (IRC Section 6015(c))

This relief separates the understatement of tax between you and your spouse, making each responsible only for their portion. To qualify:

  • You filed a joint return
  • You're divorced, legally separated, widowed, or haven't lived with your spouse in the past 12 months
  • You didn't have actual knowledge of the items causing the understatement

3. Equitable Relief (IRC Section 6015(f))

If you don't qualify for the first two types, you may still receive relief if it would be inequitable to hold you liable. This is the most flexible option and can apply to underpayments (tax was correctly reported but not paid) as well as understatements.

The IRS considers factors such as:

  • Your marital status and whether you're divorced or separated
  • Whether you would suffer economic hardship if relief isn't granted
  • Whether you knew or had reason to know about the tax liability
  • Whether you significantly benefited from the unpaid taxes
  • Whether you were abused by your spouse
  • Your current financial situation

How to Request Innocent Spouse Relief

To request relief, you must file Form 8857 (Request for Innocent Spouse Relief). Here's what to expect:

  1. Gather documentation: Collect evidence supporting your claim, including financial records, correspondence, and anything showing you didn't know about or benefit from the tax issues
  2. Complete Form 8857: Provide detailed information about your marriage, finances, and the tax years in question
  3. Submit supporting statement: Write a detailed explanation of why you deserve relief
  4. Wait for IRS contact: The IRS will notify your spouse, who has 60 days to respond
  5. Receive determination: Processing typically takes 6-12 months

When Should You Consider Innocent Spouse Relief?

Consider requesting relief if:

  • Your spouse failed to report income you didn't know about
  • Your spouse claimed false deductions or credits
  • Your spouse committed tax fraud without your knowledge
  • You signed a return under duress or without understanding
  • Your spouse handled all financial matters during the marriage
  • You're being held responsible for a tax debt that was supposed to be your ex's responsibility per divorce decree

Special Considerations for Abuse Survivors

If you experienced domestic abuse or coercion, the IRS gives special consideration to your case. You may have signed returns without full knowledge or under threat. Document any abuse and inform the IRS, as this can significantly impact your eligibility for relief.

Key Points to Remember

Protection Available

You may not be responsible for tax debt caused by your spouse's errors or fraud, even if you signed the joint return.

Three Options

Traditional relief, Separation of Liability, and Equitable Relief each have different requirements and benefits.

Time Limits Apply

Most relief must be requested within 2 years of the first collection attempt, but Equitable Relief has no deadline.

Spouse Notified

The IRS will notify your spouse of your request. They have the right to participate in the review process.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the three types of Innocent Spouse Relief?

The IRS offers three types: 1) Innocent Spouse Relief under IRC Section 6015(b) for understatements of tax due to erroneous items from your spouse, 2) Separation of Liability Relief under IRC Section 6015(c) which allocates the tax liability between you and your spouse, and 3) Equitable Relief under IRC Section 6015(f) when you don't qualify for the other two types but it would be unfair to hold you liable.

Do I have to be divorced to request Innocent Spouse Relief?

No, you do not have to be divorced. However, the type of relief available may differ based on your marital status. Separation of Liability Relief requires that you are divorced, legally separated, widowed, or have not lived with your spouse for at least 12 months. Innocent Spouse Relief and Equitable Relief are available regardless of your current marital status.

How long do I have to file for Innocent Spouse Relief?

You generally must file Form 8857 (Request for Innocent Spouse Relief) within 2 years after the IRS first attempts to collect the tax from you. However, for Equitable Relief, there is no 2-year deadline - you can request it as long as the IRS collection period remains open (typically 10 years from assessment).

Will the IRS notify my spouse if I request Innocent Spouse Relief?

Yes, by law the IRS must notify your spouse or former spouse when you file for Innocent Spouse Relief. Your spouse has the right to participate in the process and provide information. However, if you're a victim of domestic abuse or have safety concerns, inform the IRS and they may be able to take precautions.

What happens if my Innocent Spouse Relief request is denied?

If denied, you have 30 days to request an administrative appeal with the IRS Office of Appeals. If the appeal is unsuccessful, you can petition the U.S. Tax Court within 90 days of the final determination. Working with a tax professional can help strengthen your case for appeal and ensure proper procedures are followed.
