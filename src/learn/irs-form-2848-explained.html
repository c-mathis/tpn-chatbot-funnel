What Is IRS Form 2848?

IRS Form 2848, officially titled "Power of Attorney and Declaration of Representative," is a legal document that authorizes a qualified individual to represent you before the Internal Revenue Service. When you sign this form, you're giving someone permission to act on your behalf in tax matters.

Having professional representation can be invaluable when dealing with IRS issues. Your representative can communicate with the IRS, negotiate payment arrangements, attend meetings and hearings, and receive confidential tax information - all without requiring your direct involvement in every interaction.

Who Can Represent You Before the IRS?

Not just anyone can represent you before the IRS. Only individuals who meet specific qualifications are eligible:

Unlimited Representation Rights

  • Attorneys: Licensed to practice law in any U.S. jurisdiction.
  • Certified Public Accountants (CPAs): Licensed by any state board of accountancy.
  • Enrolled Agents (EAs): Licensed by the IRS after passing a comprehensive exam or through former IRS employee status.

These professionals can represent you on any tax matter before any IRS office.

Limited Representation Rights

  • Enrolled Retirement Plan Agents: Can represent on retirement plan matters.
  • Enrolled Actuaries: Can represent on certain actuarial issues.
  • Annual Filing Season Program Participants: Can represent only clients whose returns they prepared, and only before certain IRS personnel.

Special Circumstances

  • Family Members: Immediate family (spouse, parent, child, sibling) can represent you, but only before revenue agents, customer service representatives, and similar IRS employees - not in Appeals or Tax Court.
  • Return Preparers: Unenrolled preparers can represent clients only for returns they prepared and signed.
  • Individuals with Special Authorization: In specific situations, the IRS may authorize others to represent you.

What Authority Does Form 2848 Grant?

When properly completed, Form 2848 authorizes your representative to:

  • Receive and inspect your confidential tax information
  • Speak with the IRS on your behalf
  • Sign agreements, consents, and other documents
  • Receive refund checks (if specifically authorized)
  • Substitute or add another representative (if not specifically prohibited)
  • Handle correspondence and notices
  • Attend meetings, audits, and hearings in your place

How to Complete Form 2848

Part I: Power of Attorney

Line 1: Taxpayer Information

Enter your name, address, Social Security Number or EIN, daytime telephone number, and fax number. If filing jointly, include information for both spouses.

Line 2: Representative Information

For each representative, provide their name, address, CAF number (Centralized Authorization File number - they'll have this), PTIN (if applicable), telephone number, fax number, and designation (attorney, CPA, enrolled agent, etc.).

Line 3: Tax Matters

Specify exactly what tax matters are covered:

  • Type of Tax: Income, employment, estate, gift, etc.
  • Tax Form Number: 1040, 941, 1120, etc.
  • Year(s) or Period(s): Specific years or quarters covered (e.g., "2020, 2021, 2022" or "all years")

Be specific - the IRS will only recognize authority for the exact matters listed.

Line 4: Specific Use (Optional)

Check this box only if the POA is for a specific use not recorded on the CAF. Most taxpayers leave this blank.

Line 5: Acts Authorized

Your representative automatically receives standard authorities unless you modify them. You can specifically authorize or exclude:

  • Signing consents to extend statutes of limitation
  • Signing waivers
  • Executing closing agreements
  • Receiving (but not endorsing) refund checks
  • Substituting or adding representatives

Line 6: Retention/Revocation

Check if you want to keep previous POAs in effect. Otherwise, filing a new Form 2848 automatically revokes prior POAs for the same tax matters.

Line 7: Signature

Sign and date the form. For joint returns, both spouses must sign if both are granting authority. The signature must be your own - your representative cannot sign for you on this form.

Part II: Declaration of Representative

Your representative completes this section, declaring their qualifications to represent you. They must sign under penalties of perjury, confirming they are authorized to practice before the IRS.

Form 2848 vs. Form 8821

These forms serve different purposes:

Feature Form 2848 (POA) Form 8821 (TIA)
Receive tax information Yes Yes
Represent before IRS Yes No
Sign documents for taxpayer Yes (if authorized) No
Make decisions on taxpayer's behalf Yes No
Who can be appointed Qualified practitioners only Anyone

How to Submit Form 2848

You have several options for filing:

  • Online via Tax Pro Account: Representatives can submit POAs electronically through their IRS Tax Pro Account. This is the fastest method.
  • Fax: Send to the IRS CAF unit for your state. Processing takes about 5 business days.
  • Mail: Send to the IRS address for your state. Allow several weeks for processing.
  • Attach to return: Include with a tax return being filed.

Important Considerations

  • Multiple representatives: You can appoint more than one representative. List all of them on the same Form 2848.
  • Prior authorizations: Unless you check the box to retain them, a new Form 2848 revokes prior POAs for the same matters.
  • Revocation: You can revoke a POA at any time by submitting a written statement to the IRS CAF unit.
  • Representative withdrawal: Your representative can withdraw by filing Form 2848 marked "WITHDRAW."
  • Keep copies: Always maintain copies of the signed Form 2848 for your records.

Key Points to Remember

Qualified Representatives Only

Only attorneys, CPAs, enrolled agents, and certain other qualified individuals can have full representation authority.

Specific Tax Matters

Form 2848 only applies to the exact tax types, forms, and years you specify. Be thorough when completing Line 3.

Full Representation Power

Unlike Form 8821, Form 2848 allows your representative to act on your behalf, not just receive information.

Revocable Anytime

You maintain control and can revoke the POA at any time by submitting written notice to the IRS.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is IRS Form 2848?

IRS Form 2848 is the Power of Attorney and Declaration of Representative form that authorizes a qualified individual to represent you before the Internal Revenue Service. When you sign this form, you give someone permission to speak on your behalf, receive confidential tax information, sign agreements, and handle tax matters for you.

Who can represent me before the IRS with Form 2848?

Only certain individuals can represent you with full authority: attorneys, CPAs, enrolled agents, enrolled retirement plan agents, and enrolled actuaries. In limited circumstances, family members or individuals involved in preparing your return can also represent you, but only before certain IRS personnel. Each representative must sign Part II of Form 2848 declaring their qualifications.

What is the difference between Form 2848 and Form 8821?

Form 2848 grants full representation authority - your representative can act on your behalf, make decisions, sign documents, and communicate with the IRS for you. Form 8821 (Tax Information Authorization) only allows someone to receive and inspect your confidential tax information but not represent you or take any actions on your behalf.

How long does Form 2848 remain valid?

Form 2848 remains valid until you revoke it, the representative withdraws, or a new Form 2848 is submitted that supersedes it for the same tax matters. There is no automatic expiration date, but you can specify one on the form if you want the authority to end on a certain date.

Can I limit the authority granted on Form 2848?

Yes, you can customize Form 2848 to limit the authority granted. You specify which tax forms and tax years are covered on Line 3. You can also exclude certain powers on Line 5, such as signing agreements, receiving refund checks, or substituting representatives. Any limitations should be clearly written on the form.
