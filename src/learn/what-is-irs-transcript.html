An IRS transcript is an official document that summarizes information from your tax returns and IRS account. Unlike a copy of your actual tax return, transcripts are free, readily available, and contain most of the information needed for tax relief applications, loan applications, and verifying your tax history.

Types of IRS Transcripts

The IRS offers five different types of transcripts, each serving a specific purpose:

Tax Return Transcript

This transcript shows most line items from your original tax return as you filed it, including any accompanying forms and schedules. It does not reflect any changes made after filing. Tax Return Transcripts are commonly requested by mortgage lenders and financial institutions for income verification.

Availability: Current year and past 3 years

Tax Account Transcript

The Account Transcript shows basic return information plus any subsequent adjustments, including penalties, interest, payments, and the current balance owed. This is the most useful transcript for tax relief purposes because it shows your complete payment and assessment history.

Availability: Current year and up to 10 prior years

Record of Account Transcript

This is a combination of the Tax Return Transcript and Tax Account Transcript. It provides the most comprehensive view of your tax account, showing both the original return information and all subsequent activity.

Availability: Current year and past 3 years

Wage and Income Transcript

This transcript shows the income reported to the IRS by employers, banks, and other payers through forms like W-2s, 1099s, and 1098s. It is extremely useful for preparing unfiled tax returns or verifying all income sources.

Availability: Current year and up to 10 prior years

Verification of Non-Filing Letter

This letter confirms that the IRS has no record of a filed tax return for a specific year. It may be required for certain financial aid applications or to prove to a lender that you were not required to file.

Availability: Current year and past 3 years

How to Request IRS Transcripts

Online via IRS.gov

The fastest way to get transcripts is through the IRS Get Transcript Online tool at irs.gov. You will need to:

  1. Create an account or sign in with ID.me
  2. Complete identity verification (requires a photo ID, selfie, and personal information)
  3. Select the type of transcript you need
  4. Choose the tax year
  5. View or download immediately

By Phone

Call the IRS automated phone transcript service at 1-800-908-9946. You can request transcripts be mailed to your address on file. Allow 5-10 business days for delivery.

By Mail

Complete Form 4506-T (Request for Transcript of Tax Return) and mail it to the IRS. Processing takes approximately 5-10 business days for delivery, though it can take longer during peak tax season.

Through a Tax Professional

If you have authorized a tax professional with a Power of Attorney (Form 2848), they can request transcripts on your behalf through the IRS Practitioner Priority Service or e-Services portal. Tax professionals often have faster access and can obtain additional information not available to taxpayers directly.

Understanding Your Transcript

IRS transcripts use codes to describe account activity. Here are some common transaction codes you may see:

  • Code 150: Tax return filed and tax liability assessed
  • Code 806: Credit for federal tax withheld (from W-2s)
  • Code 766: Credit applied to your account (refund, payment, etc.)
  • Code 846: Refund issued
  • Code 971: Notice issued by IRS
  • Code 570: Additional account action pending
  • Code 290: Additional tax assessed
  • Code 300: Additional tax or penalty assessment

Why Transcripts Matter for Tax Relief

When applying for tax relief programs like an Offer in Compromise or installment agreement, transcripts provide critical information:

  • Verify balances owed: Account transcripts show exactly what you owe for each tax year
  • Identify penalties and interest: See how much of your balance is principal versus penalties and interest (important for penalty abatement requests)
  • Find assessment dates: Determine the Collection Statute Expiration Date for each tax year
  • Confirm compliance: Verify all returns have been filed and all income has been reported
  • Track payments: See all payments credited to your account

Before pursuing any tax relief option, getting your transcripts is typically the first step. They provide a clear picture of where you stand with the IRS and help identify the best resolution strategy for your situation.

Key Points to Remember

Always Free

IRS transcripts are completely free to obtain online, by phone, or by mail. No fees required.

Multiple Types

Five transcript types serve different purposes. Account Transcripts are most useful for tax relief situations.

Instant Online Access

Get transcripts immediately through IRS.gov after completing identity verification with ID.me.

Tax Pro Access

Authorized representatives can obtain transcripts faster through IRS Practitioner Priority Service.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a tax transcript and a tax return?

A tax return is the actual form you filed with the IRS. A tax transcript is a summary document created by the IRS that shows the key information from your return. Transcripts are free and easy to obtain, while getting a copy of your actual return requires Form 4506 and a fee.

How do I get my IRS transcript online?

You can get transcripts online through the IRS Get Transcript Online tool at irs.gov. You will need to create an ID.me account to verify your identity. Once verified, you can view and download transcripts immediately for free.

What type of transcript do I need for tax relief?

For tax relief purposes, you typically need an Account Transcript, which shows your tax liability, payments, penalties, interest, and balance. A tax professional may also request a Wage and Income Transcript to verify all income sources and ensure compliance.

How far back can I get IRS transcripts?

Tax Return Transcripts are available for the current year and the past 3 years. Account Transcripts are generally available for the current year and up to 10 prior years. Wage and Income Transcripts are available for the current year and up to 10 prior years.

Are IRS transcripts free?

Yes, IRS transcripts are completely free. You can request them online, by phone, or by mail at no cost. However, if you need an actual copy of your tax return (not a transcript), you must file Form 4506 and pay a $43 fee per return.
