What Is IRS Form 843?

IRS Form 843, officially titled "Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement," is a versatile form used to ask the IRS to reduce or eliminate certain penalties and interest, or to refund taxes, fees, or other amounts that were incorrectly collected. It's one of the primary tools for taxpayers seeking penalty relief.

While the IRS assesses penalties to encourage compliance, it also recognizes that taxpayers sometimes face circumstances beyond their control. Form 843 provides a formal way to explain why you deserve relief and request that penalties be removed from your account.

When to Use Form 843

Form 843 is appropriate for several situations:

  • Penalty abatement: Requesting removal of failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, accuracy-related, or other penalties
  • Interest abatement: Requesting reduction of interest charges in specific circumstances (very limited)
  • Refund of penalties paid: Getting back penalties you've already paid if you have grounds for relief
  • Refund of other taxes: Claiming refunds for taxes like FICA when you've overpaid
  • Fee refunds: Recovering certain fees that were improperly assessed

Note: Form 843 is not used to request abatement of income tax itself. For disputing your tax liability, you would use other procedures like filing an amended return (Form 1040-X) or pursuing formal appeals.

Understanding Penalty Abatement

The IRS may grant penalty abatement based on several criteria:

Reasonable Cause

This is the most common basis for penalty relief. You must demonstrate that you exercised ordinary business care and prudence but were still unable to comply with your tax obligations due to circumstances beyond your control. Examples include:

  • Serious illness or hospitalization of yourself or an immediate family member
  • Death of an immediate family member
  • Natural disaster or casualty (fire, flood, tornado)
  • Inability to obtain necessary records despite reasonable efforts
  • Reliance on erroneous written advice from the IRS
  • IRS errors or delays that contributed to your non-compliance
  • Unavoidable absence from the country

First-Time Penalty Abatement (FTA)

This administrative waiver provides relief for taxpayers with clean compliance records. To qualify:

  • You have no penalties (other than estimated tax penalties) for the prior three tax years
  • You've filed all required returns or filed valid extensions
  • You've paid, or arranged to pay, any tax currently due

FTA applies to failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, and failure-to-deposit penalties. It's often the easiest form of relief to obtain because you don't need to prove reasonable cause - just a clean history.

Statutory Exception

Certain situations automatically qualify for penalty relief under tax law, such as serving in a combat zone or disaster area where the IRS has postponed filing deadlines.

How to Complete Form 843

Lines 1-2: Taxpayer Information

Enter your name, address, and either Social Security Number (for individuals) or Employer Identification Number (for businesses). Include your daytime phone number where the IRS can reach you with questions.

Line 3: Claim Amount

Enter the total dollar amount of penalties, interest, or fees you're requesting to be abated or refunded. Be specific - if you're requesting abatement of multiple penalties, calculate and list each one.

Line 4: Type of Tax

Specify the type of tax related to your request, such as "Income Tax," "Employment Tax," "FICA," or "Estate Tax."

Line 5: Tax Periods

List the specific tax periods for which you're requesting relief. For income tax, this is typically the calendar year (e.g., "2022"). For payroll taxes, include the specific quarters (e.g., "3rd Quarter 2023").

Line 6: Type of Penalty

Check the appropriate box indicating what type of penalty you're addressing. Options include failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, accuracy-related, and others.

Line 7: Detailed Explanation

This is the most important part of your request. Provide a clear, detailed explanation of:

  • What happened that prevented you from complying
  • Why the circumstances were beyond your control
  • What steps you took to try to comply despite the circumstances
  • How you've since become compliant

If space is insufficient, attach a separate statement marked "Form 843 - Continuation of Line 7."

Supporting Documentation

Strengthen your request with relevant evidence:

  • Medical records: Hospital records, doctor's statements documenting illness or disability
  • Death certificates: For claims based on family member death
  • Fire or police reports: For casualty-related claims
  • Insurance claims: Documentation of natural disasters
  • Written IRS correspondence: If claiming reliance on IRS advice
  • Tax professional statements: If claiming preparer errors
  • Payment records: Showing you've addressed the underlying tax issue

Where to File Form 843

Mail Form 843 to the IRS service center where you would normally file your tax return, or to the address on the IRS notice assessing the penalty. Include:

  • The completed Form 843
  • All supporting documentation
  • A copy of the IRS notice showing the penalty (if applicable)
  • Form 2848 if a representative is handling your case

What Happens After Filing

After the IRS receives your Form 843:

  1. Initial review: The IRS checks that your form is complete and properly filed.
  2. Examination: An IRS employee reviews your explanation and supporting documents.
  3. Decision: You'll receive a written response either granting, partially granting, or denying your request.
  4. Appeal rights: If denied, you have the right to appeal the decision.

Processing typically takes 6 to 8 weeks, though complex cases may take longer.

Tips for a Successful Request

  • Be specific: Vague explanations like "I was sick" are less effective than specific details with dates and circumstances.
  • Show the connection: Explain exactly how your circumstances prevented compliance.
  • Demonstrate good faith: Show that you tried to comply and have since corrected the issue.
  • Document everything: Include all relevant supporting evidence.
  • Be honest: Never fabricate or exaggerate circumstances.
  • Consider professional help: For significant penalty amounts, a tax professional may improve your chances.

Key Points to Remember

Significant Savings

IRS penalties can add 25% or more to your tax bill. Successful abatement can save thousands of dollars.

First-Time Abatement

Clean compliance history? You may qualify for automatic penalty removal without proving reasonable cause.

Documentation Matters

Strong supporting evidence significantly increases your chances of approval. Include all relevant documents.

6-8 Week Processing

Expect the IRS to take several weeks to review your request. Complex cases may take longer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is IRS Form 843?

IRS Form 843 is used to request a refund of certain taxes, interest, penalties, or fees that were erroneously or illegally assessed, or to request abatement (removal) of penalties and interest. It's commonly used for penalty relief requests when taxpayers have reasonable cause for non-compliance or qualify for first-time penalty abatement.

What types of penalties can be abated with Form 843?

Form 843 can be used to request abatement of failure-to-file penalties, failure-to-pay penalties, failure-to-deposit penalties (for payroll taxes), accuracy-related penalties, and various other IRS penalties. You must demonstrate reasonable cause for the non-compliance or meet the criteria for first-time penalty abatement.

What is reasonable cause for penalty abatement?

Reasonable cause includes circumstances beyond your control that prevented tax compliance, such as serious illness, natural disaster, death of an immediate family member, inability to obtain records despite reasonable efforts, fire or other casualty, IRS errors, or reliance on incorrect written advice from the IRS.

What is first-time penalty abatement?

First-time penalty abatement (FTA) is an administrative waiver that removes failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, or failure-to-deposit penalties for taxpayers with a clean compliance history. To qualify, you must have filed all required returns and have no penalties (other than estimated tax penalties) in the prior three tax years.

How long does the IRS take to process Form 843?

Processing times vary, but the IRS typically takes 6 to 8 weeks to process Form 843 requests. Complex cases or requests requiring additional review may take longer. You'll receive a written response either approving, partially approving, or denying your request. If denied, you have appeal rights.
