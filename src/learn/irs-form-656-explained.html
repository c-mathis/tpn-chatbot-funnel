What Is IRS Form 656?

IRS Form 656, officially titled "Offer in Compromise," is the cornerstone document for taxpayers seeking to settle their federal tax debt for less than the full amount owed. This form allows you to make a formal proposal to the IRS, offering a specific dollar amount to resolve your outstanding tax liability.

The Offer in Compromise program exists because the IRS recognizes that some taxpayers simply cannot pay their full tax debt. Rather than pursue collection efforts indefinitely, the IRS may accept a reduced amount when it represents the most they can reasonably expect to collect.

When Should You File Form 656?

Form 656 is appropriate when you genuinely cannot afford to pay your full tax liability. The IRS accepts offers based on three grounds:

  • Doubt as to Collectibility: The most common basis. You prove that your income, expenses, and assets make it impossible to pay the full amount before the collection statute expires.
  • Doubt as to Liability: You legitimately dispute that you owe the tax amount assessed. This requires strong documentation showing the IRS made an error.
  • Effective Tax Administration: You technically could pay, but doing so would create exceptional hardship or would be unfair due to special circumstances.

How to Complete Form 656: Step-by-Step

Section 1: Taxpayer Information

Provide your full legal name, Social Security Number, and current address. If filing jointly with a spouse, both parties must be listed and both must sign the form. Business taxpayers use the EIN instead of SSN.

Section 2: Tax Periods and Amounts

List each tax year and type of tax you're addressing. Be specific about which tax periods your offer covers. The IRS will only consider forgiveness for the periods explicitly listed on your form. You can obtain exact amounts from IRS transcripts.

Section 3: Basis for Your Offer

Check the appropriate box indicating why you're submitting the offer. Most taxpayers select "Doubt as to Collectibility" because they cannot afford to pay. If you believe you don't owe the tax, select "Doubt as to Liability" and attach Form 656-L with supporting documentation.

Section 4: Payment Terms

Choose between two payment options:

  • Lump Sum Cash Offer: Pay your offer in 5 or fewer payments within 5 months of acceptance. You must include 20% of your total offer amount with Form 656 as a non-refundable initial payment.
  • Periodic Payment Offer: Pay your offer in monthly installments over 6 to 24 months. Include your first proposed monthly payment with the form and continue making payments while the IRS reviews your application.

Section 5: Offer Amount

Enter the total dollar amount you're offering. This should be based on your calculated "Reasonable Collection Potential" (RCP) from Form 433-A (OIC). The IRS generally won't accept less than your RCP, which includes the equity in your assets plus your expected future income over a specified period.

Section 6: Source of Funds

Explain where your offer payment will come from. Common sources include savings, loans from family, borrowing against assets, or future income. The IRS wants to verify that your offer is realistic and achievable.

Required Supporting Documents

Form 656 alone is not enough. You must submit a complete OIC package including:

  • Form 433-A (OIC): Required for individuals. This financial statement details your income, expenses, assets, and liabilities.
  • Form 433-B (OIC): Required if you own a business or are self-employed.
  • Application fee: $205 payment (check, money order, or card).
  • Initial payment: 20% of lump sum offers or first monthly payment for periodic offers.
  • Proof of income: Pay stubs, profit and loss statements, Social Security statements.
  • Bank statements: Three months of statements for all accounts.
  • Asset documentation: Vehicle valuations, property assessments, retirement account statements.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many OIC applications are rejected for preventable errors:

  • Missing signatures: Both spouses must sign if filing jointly.
  • Unfiled tax returns: You must be current on all required filings before the IRS will consider your offer.
  • Incomplete financial forms: Blank fields or missing documentation leads to rejection.
  • Lowball offers: Offering less than your calculated RCP without justification will result in rejection.
  • Forgetting estimated payments: Self-employed individuals must be current on estimated tax payments for the current year.

What Happens After You Submit

Once the IRS receives your complete OIC package, you'll receive acknowledgment within a few weeks. An IRS examiner will be assigned to review your finances and verify the information provided. They may request additional documentation or clarification.

During the review period (typically 6-12 months), the IRS generally suspends collection activities. However, the 10-year collection statute is also suspended plus 30 days. If your offer is accepted, you must comply with all tax filing and payment requirements for 5 years, or the IRS can default your agreement and reinstate the original debt.

Key Points to Remember

$205 Application Fee

The non-refundable fee is required with your submission. Low-income taxpayers (below 250% of poverty guidelines) may qualify for a waiver.

20% Down Payment

Lump sum offers require 20% upfront. Periodic payment offers require your first monthly payment with the application.

6-12 Month Process

The IRS takes time to review your financial situation. Collection activities are typically suspended during review.

5-Year Compliance

After acceptance, you must file and pay all taxes on time for 5 years to keep your offer in good standing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is IRS Form 656?

IRS Form 656 is the official Offer in Compromise application form used to propose settling your tax debt for less than the full amount owed. It requires detailed information about your finances and the specific amount you're offering to pay. This form is part of a complete OIC package that includes financial statements and supporting documentation.

How much does it cost to file Form 656?

The IRS charges a $205 application fee for Form 656. Additionally, you must include an initial payment with your offer - either 20% of a lump sum offer or the first monthly payment for a periodic payment offer. Low-income taxpayers (those at or below 250% of federal poverty guidelines) may qualify for fee waivers by completing the certification on Form 656.

What forms do I need to submit with Form 656?

Along with Form 656, you must submit Form 433-A (OIC) if you're an individual, Form 433-B (OIC) if you have a business, the $205 application fee, your initial payment, and supporting documentation including pay stubs, bank statements, and asset valuations. The IRS provides a complete checklist in the Form 656 Booklet.

How long does the IRS take to process Form 656?

The IRS typically takes 6 to 12 months to review and process an Offer in Compromise application. Complex cases may take longer. During this review period, the IRS generally suspends collection activities on your account, giving you time to focus on the application process.

What happens if my Form 656 offer is rejected?

If your offer is rejected, you have 30 days to file an appeal with the IRS Independent Office of Appeals. You can also submit a new offer with different terms, explore installment agreements, or request currently not collectible status depending on your situation. Working with a tax professional can help identify why your offer was rejected and improve your chances on appeal or resubmission.
