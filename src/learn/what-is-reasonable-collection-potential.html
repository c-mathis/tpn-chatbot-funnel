Reasonable Collection Potential (RCP) represents the IRS's estimate of how much they can realistically collect from you over the remaining time on the collection statute. When you submit an Offer in Compromise, the IRS uses RCP to evaluate whether your offer represents the most they could collect through other means. Your offer must generally equal or exceed your RCP to be accepted.

The RCP Formula

The IRS calculates RCP using two main components:

RCP = Net Equity in Assets + Future Income

Let's break down each component:

Net Equity in Assets

This is the value of everything you own that the IRS could potentially seize to satisfy your debt, including:

  • Real estate (primary residence, investment properties)
  • Vehicles (cars, motorcycles, boats, RVs)
  • Bank accounts and cash
  • Investments (stocks, bonds, mutual funds)
  • Retirement accounts (IRA, 401k - given special consideration)
  • Life insurance cash value
  • Business assets and equipment
  • Personal property of value (jewelry, collectibles, etc.)

The IRS applies a "quick sale value" (QSV) to most assets, typically 80% of fair market value, to account for the cost and difficulty of liquidating assets quickly. Then they subtract any outstanding loans or encumbrances.

Example: If your home is worth $300,000 and you owe $250,000 on the mortgage:

  • Quick Sale Value: $300,000 x 80% = $240,000
  • Less Mortgage: $240,000 - $250,000 = -$10,000
  • Net Equity: $0 (negative equity counts as zero)

Future Income

The second component estimates how much money you could pay toward your tax debt over time. It is calculated as:

Future Income = Monthly Disposable Income x Multiplier

Monthly Disposable Income is your gross monthly income minus your allowable living expenses. The IRS uses standardized expense allowances for categories like:

  • Food, clothing, housekeeping supplies, personal care
  • Housing and utilities (based on local standards)
  • Transportation (ownership costs and operating costs)
  • Health care
  • Other necessary expenses (varies by situation)

The Multiplier depends on your payment option:

  • Lump Sum Offer: 12 months (paid in 5 months or less after acceptance)
  • Periodic Payment Offer: 24 months (paid in 6-24 months after acceptance)

Complete RCP Calculation Example

Let's walk through a complete example for someone with $50,000 in tax debt:

Assets

Home Equity (QSV minus mortgage) $0
Vehicle Equity (2018 Honda, $8,000 QSV minus $5,000 loan) $3,000
Bank Accounts $2,000
Retirement (usually discounted significantly) $0
Total Net Equity in Assets $5,000

Income and Expenses (Monthly)

Gross Monthly Income $5,500
Less: Allowable Expenses (per IRS standards) -$5,200
Monthly Disposable Income $300

RCP Calculation

Net Equity in Assets $5,000
Future Income ($300 x 12 months for lump sum) $3,600
Minimum Offer Amount (RCP) $8,600

In this example, the taxpayer could potentially settle $50,000 in tax debt for approximately $8,600.

Factors That Affect Your RCP

Several factors can increase or decrease your RCP:

Factors That Increase RCP

  • High equity in assets
  • Significant disposable income after allowable expenses
  • Expenses that exceed IRS standards (reducing your allowable deductions)
  • Recent increases in income
  • Assets transferred to others (IRS may include dissipated assets)

Factors That May Decrease RCP

  • Medical conditions that affect earning potential
  • Age (shorter remaining working years)
  • Documented necessary expenses above IRS standards
  • Care for elderly parents or disabled family members
  • Education expenses for children
  • Court-ordered payments (child support, alimony)

Why Professional Help Matters

Calculating RCP accurately requires detailed knowledge of IRS standards and proper documentation. Common mistakes that can result in a rejected offer or unnecessarily high offer amount include:

  • Incorrect asset valuations
  • Missing legitimate expense deductions
  • Not accounting for allowable adjustments
  • Using incorrect expense standards for your area
  • Failing to document special circumstances

A qualified tax professional can help ensure your RCP is calculated correctly and present your case in the most favorable light while remaining compliant with IRS requirements.

Key Points to Remember

Minimum Offer Amount

Your Offer in Compromise must generally equal or exceed your RCP to be accepted by the IRS.

Two Components

RCP equals net equity in assets plus future income (monthly disposable income x 12 or 24 months).

IRS Standards Apply

The IRS uses standardized expense allowances, not your actual spending, to calculate disposable income.

Quick Sale Value

Assets are valued at 80% of fair market value to account for the cost of a forced sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the RCP formula?

The basic RCP formula is: Net Equity in Assets + (Monthly Disposable Income x Future Income Multiplier) = RCP. The future income multiplier is 12 for lump sum offers (paid in 5 months or less) or 24 for periodic payment offers (paid in 6-24 months).

How does the IRS calculate net equity in assets?

The IRS determines net equity by taking the fair market value of your assets and subtracting any loans or encumbrances. For real estate, vehicles, and some other assets, the IRS applies a quick sale value (typically 80% of fair market value) to account for the cost of a forced sale. Certain assets like retirement accounts may be given additional consideration.

What is monthly disposable income?

Monthly disposable income is your gross monthly income minus your allowable living expenses as determined by IRS standards. The IRS uses national and local standards for housing, transportation, food, and other necessities. If you have income left over after these allowable expenses, that is your disposable income.

Why is my RCP higher than what I can actually pay?

The RCP formula uses IRS allowable expense standards, not your actual spending. If you spend more on housing, transportation, or other categories than the IRS allows, your calculated disposable income will be higher than what you actually have left over. Working with a tax professional can help identify legitimate adjustments and present your case effectively.

Can I offer less than my calculated RCP?

Generally, the IRS will not accept an offer below your RCP unless you can demonstrate special circumstances. However, accurate asset valuation, proper application of allowable expenses, and consideration of health issues or other factors can legitimately reduce your RCP. This is where professional assistance is valuable.
